Tarantulas

Tarantulas are arachnids that belong to the family Theraphosidae, and are found all over the world. However, not all tarantulas are the same. They are divided into two main categories: Old World and New World.

New World tarantulas originate from the Americas, including the Southern United States and Caribbean Islands. They differ from Old World tarantulas in that they use uricating setae, barbed stinging hairs located on their upper abdomens, as their main line of defense. These hairs can be kicked off by many species at predators, causing significant irritation. However, this is not their main function. Both Old World and New World tarantulas have surprisingly poor eyesight and mainly use their setae as sensory organs to understand their environment.

Old World tarantulas are located in the Eastern Hemisphere. They are considered more difficult to care for due to their size, speed, and defensive nature. Old World tarantulas rely on their bite often as their primary line of defense if threatened, although they will most likely use their advanced speed and agility before their bite. These spiders have medically significant venom, meaning a bite can cause some serious problems. Although still kept by people in the hobby, it is advised that only advanced keepers keep these types of tarantulas.
